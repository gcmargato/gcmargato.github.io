Imagining the future, navigating memories

July 2026

Despite being central to the articulation of what could be presented as a not integrated global history on the production and organization of social differences, casta and raça were the two central concepts in the articulation of Professor Rao’s thinking during the 2,5 days that she spend promoting debate at University of Lisbon Social Sciences Institute.

Being an expert on anglophone contexts or former British colonies, Rao articulated the course in the permanent tension between the global and the particular. Her narrative was permanently about complexity, suggesting the question, but not able to overcome those articulations of social difference.

Following the ongoing trends on critical theory, Anupama Rao defended the reconstruction of the archive, centring a considerable part of the discussion on Hartman’s «critical fabulation». However, Rao called our attention to the particular nature of the archive that allowed Hartman’s way of relating with the history.

However, the thing that put me thinking was an intervention made by a colleague. Drawing upon the paradigm of emigration-as-resistance, he criticized the Aljube Museum excessive focus on the Portuguese emigration towards Europe, ignoring previous migration paths. His argument was that this emphasis and the silencing of previous migrations towards non-European spaces, could point towards a Eurocentric imagination of the nation.

Despite generally agreeing on the mobilization of several repertoires in order to legitimize the Portuguese (disputed) Europeanity, I would like to use this example to identify other axis on the articulation of Portuguese autobiography . First, the Portuguese emigration towards European countries is part of a new post-WW2 global history, while the emigration towards the Brazil belongs to other moment, between the late-19th century and the first half of the 20th century.

In fact, there is (or there was) a significant public memory of these emigrants, profoundly embedded in a rural imaginary. It gave place to a canção offered to the famous fado singer Amália, but sung and popularized by Alexandra: « Zé Brasileiro, Português de Braga » [Brazilian Zé, Portuguese from Braga]. The lyrics describes the difficulties of this emigrants’ lives.
